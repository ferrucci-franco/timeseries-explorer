# Time Series Explorer v0.4.0

Climate grids on a map — ERA5, CMIP and GRIB, read by parts however large — the whole app on a phone, data tools that run over every row of a file too big for memory, and tables read the way they were written.

## Downloads

- **Windows 10/11 x64:** installer and portable EXE.
- **macOS Intel:** DMG installer and ZIP package.
- **macOS Apple silicon:** DMG installer and ZIP package.
- **Linux x86_64:** DEB installer and AppImage package.
- **SHA256SUMS files:** checksums for verifying the downloaded packages.

The executables are currently unsigned. Verify that the file was downloaded from the official Time Series Explorer download button and compare its SHA-256 checksum before running it.

- **Windows:** SmartScreen may show an **Unknown publisher** warning. Choose *More info* → *Run anyway*.
- **macOS:** Gatekeeper refuses an unsigned, un-notarized app with *"Time Series Explorer" cannot be opened*. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll to the message naming the app, and click **Open Anyway**; then confirm in the dialog that follows. On macOS Sequoia this panel is the only route — right-click → Open no longer offers the override.

## Gridded data on a map

A netCDF holding a grid — an ERA5 download from the Copernicus CDS, a CMIP model run — opens on a map instead of being unrolled into thousands of series. The time series come out of it on request.

- **Read by parts, in a worker.** Only the step on screen and the cells a series needs are read, so a multi-GB file opens in about a second without the full-load question. netCDF4/HDF5, netCDF3 (classic, 64-bit offset, CDF-5) and the CDS ZIP of instantaneous and accumulated variables are all read this way, with CF time in any calendar (`noleap`, `360_day`…), packed and masked values, and descending latitudes.
- **GRIB opens too.** ERA5's native format — `.grib`, `.grb`, `.grib2`, or a GRIB saved as `.nc` as `cdsapi` scripts often do — is read by its own decoder: GRIB1 and GRIB2, simple, complex and spatially-differenced packing, bitmaps, on regular latitude/longitude grids. The values match ecCodes exactly. JPEG 2000, PNG and CCSDS packing, gaussian and spectral grids are named in the message, with the command that converts them.
- **Navigate, Point, Region.** Pan and zoom with the mouse, the wheel or a trackpad; place a point by clicking or by typing coordinates; draw a box or type it the way the CDS form asks for it (North, West, East, South). Points and boxes can be selected, dragged and removed.
- **What a point and a box mean is stated.** A point is the nearest grid cell or a bilinear interpolation of its four neighbours, drawn on the map as the stencil it reads. A box is the area-weighted mean over its cells, weighted by cos(latitude). The **?** beside the tools explains each one.
- **Levels and time.** Every pressure level, a time slider and playback that reads the next step while it draws the current one. One extraction can take several levels at once.
- **Fast on large files.** On a 1.67 GB ERA5 month, a bilinear point series takes about 5 s, a box over Europe about 6 s and a global box about 13 s. Long series are split along time between several workers.
- **The extractions are ordinary series.** Each place is a folder in the variable tree, named by what it is and where; its series plot, export and analyse like any other, and a project saves the map, its places and their values.
- **A colour bar you can fix,** under one *Color range* button, and the coastlines of the region on screen.
- Works on phones: a tap places the point, a finger drags it, and a long press opens the menu.

## Series netCDF files are read by parts as well

A netCDF of stations or a PyPSA network is parsed in a worker straight from the file, and the warning now depends on how much the series it holds would load, not on the size of the file. A 573 MB station file used to ask first and then take 18 s and 2.1 GB of memory; it now opens in 4.6 s with 350 MB, without asking.

## The app on a phone

Below 600 px wide or 500 px high the app switches to a compact layout: one plot at a time, with Data, Plot, Analyze and More as sheets on a bottom bar (a rail when the phone is sideways). It is chosen before the first paint, so the desktop layout never flashes.

- **Every plot type:** time series, 2D, 2D + t, 3D and the 2D/3D state animations. A tap in Data says which plot it goes to and assigns x, y and z.
- **Every analysis the desktop menu offers:** FFT, temporal profile, integral, histogram, heatmap, correlation and the 2D curve fit, with View and Marks as rows of the Plot sheet.
- **Measurement cursors** with 44 px tabs; ◀ ▶ step to the next sample, peak, trough or zero, and Δx and Δy stay in the app bar.
- **Gestures:** fit buttons on the plot, a slow double tap and drag to zoom to a window, two fingers to pan in 3D.
- Controls are at least 44 px and fields at least 16 px, so iOS does not zoom the page in; on/off options are switches; the keyboard no longer flips the layout.
- "Mobile version" in the ☰ menu switches to it, and More → Layout → Desktop switches back.

On any touch screen, press and hold drags a variable from the tree, one finger pans, two fingers pinch per axis, and cursors and analysis bands follow a finger.

## Files too large for memory

On a file opened in memory-saving mode, these now run over every row instead of the overview:

- formulas, through DuckDB, with every function translated — including `diff`, `cumsum` and a variable-degree `root`;
- the derivative, the detrend and the IQR;
- the integral and the time-axis index and step;
- the moving average;
- the digital filter, forward and zero-phase: on 20 million rows, 23 s and 39 s.

A new *Results in memory* setting (1024 MB by default) bounds what such results may hold. CSV export writes every row, including panels that mix large and in-memory files, and Fit Y fits the detail actually drawn.

## Limits and Parquet

- A CSV loads whole up to 300 MB (was 150), and **0 means no limit** for every limit. A whole load that runs out of memory retries in memory-saving mode.
- **Parquet works offline:** DuckDB's Parquet module ships with the app instead of being fetched on first use.

## Tables read as written

- Dates with dots (`dd.MM.yyyy`) are read as time, a units row in brackets (`[m/s]`) is recognised and keeps the column titles whole, and a *Folders* setting groups the columns. Parsing settings can be saved to a `.json` and loaded back.
- Repeated timestamps no longer cost a file its time axis: they are counted, and a *Collapse* tool merges them by mean, max, min, first or last.
- `v(V1)`-style headers keep their names, and a numeric time column's unit can be edited.

## Export

- Files that follow one another in time are joined into one CSV by default, with an optional `source_file` column.
- An FFT panel exports its spectrum.
- Export runs in chunks with progress and Cancel: a 15.9 million-sample export that froze the page for 42 s now blocks it for 18 ms.

## Derived variables

- A full-size formula editor (⤢) with live validation, variable pills and a *Combine* step that writes a sum, mean, min or max across variables. A **New variable…** button opens it.
- New functions `sum()`, `mean()` (also `avg`, `average`) and `cumsum()`, and a cumulative-sum rule in the integral tool.
- Any variable name is allowed; renaming one rewrites the formulas that use it, and dependent variables are recomputed or removed with their source.

## Plots and analyses

- A **View** menu (log scales, Stack, Y2, line shape, 2D/3D display) and a single **Analysis** menu.
- **Marks:** a NaN/Inf strip, gaps with a settable Δt, sample dots and repeated timestamps.
- Ctrl+Z / *Last view* keeps 50 steps, in every mode. A new 2D power fit, y = a·xᵇ.
- FFT: a period axis (logarithmic, with duration ticks), Fit Y to what is visible, and on data with holes it transforms the longest clean stretch instead of refusing.
- The digital filter accepts long sparse `zeros(k)` filters (up to 8192 non-zero taps) and non-causal filters.
- The hover lists up to four run parameters that differ between files, statistics count NaNs, Shift+click selects a range, and the cursor readout can be copied.
- Edge pills fold the sidebar, the analysis panels and the top bar. Stairs on 2 million samples draw in 13 ms instead of 1.45 s.
- The desktop app lets you choose its spellcheck language (not on macOS).

## Interface

- On narrow windows the top bar scrolls and the sidebar stays beside the content.
- Cancel stops the file being read: on a 134 MB CSV it takes 0.3 s in Chromium and 1.3 s in Firefox instead of 11 s. Loading examples can be cancelled too.
- Reset layout, Data tools and New variable… are off while no file is loaded.
- The portable package opened with a double click (`file://`) now runs its workers, so large netCDF and GRIB files are read by parts there too: a 1.67 GB ERA5 month shows its map in 1 s, with 160 MB in the tab instead of 1.9 GB.
- The web version is served from [www.unditas.com](https://www.unditas.com/).

## Fixes

- Load errors reach their error dialog; a reload no longer freezes and drops the variables the file no longer has.
- The time-axis summary was off by a factor of 1000.
- Cursors stay on their traces outside UTC.
- Autoscale with every trace hidden no longer loops; 3D zoom and drag work during an animation.
- In Firefox the loading dialog is no longer half-transparent and the coefficient fields scroll.
- Data-tool previews no longer rebuild the panels.

## Desktop capabilities

- Fully local, offline application and bundled processing libraries.
- External documentation links open in the operating system's default browser.
- No classic Electron menu or secondary Electron browser windows.
