# Time Series Explorer v0.4.1

The gridded map, played and handled on a phone: Play by hours, days or months at up to 30 steps a second, large ERA5 files that play at speed, fingers that pan, pinch and draw boxes in every tool, and 3D views where the mouse and the fingers do the same thing.

## Downloads

- **Windows 10/11 x64:** installer and portable EXE.
- **macOS Intel:** DMG installer and ZIP package.
- **macOS Apple silicon:** DMG installer and ZIP package.
- **Linux x86_64:** DEB installer and AppImage package.
- **SHA256SUMS files:** checksums for verifying the downloaded packages.

The executables are currently unsigned. Verify that the file was downloaded from the official Time Series Explorer download button and compare its SHA-256 checksum before running it.

- **Windows:** SmartScreen may show an **Unknown publisher** warning. Choose *More info* → *Run anyway*.
- **macOS:** Gatekeeper refuses an unsigned, un-notarized app with *"Time Series Explorer" cannot be opened*. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll to the message naming the app, and click **Open Anyway**; then confirm in the dialog that follows. On macOS Sequoia this panel is the only route — right-click → Open no longer offers the override.

## Opening netCDF and GRIB files

- The loading dialog shows from the moment a netCDF or GRIB file is chosen, and **Cancel** stops the opening at once, whatever stage it is in.
- A GRIB file opened in the browser no longer takes a minute to appear: its many small reads are served from blocks read ahead.
- The map closes when a file that is not a map is opened or selected, instead of staying over it.

## Playing the map

- **Step by a time:** besides one step of the file, Play and ◀ ▶ can move by 6 hours, a day, a week, a month or a year (or by 10, 100, 1000 steps), whichever the time axis allows. Speeds go up to 30 steps a second.
- **Large files play at speed.** The new Copernicus CDS writes long hourly requests in chunks of four months; each map read from such a file decompressed whole chunks, about 2 s per map on a year of hourly ERA5 over Argentina. Play now reads the steps it is about to show in one strided read, and keeps to the clock, skipping steps rather than slowing down when the drawing falls behind.
- **Moving the map holds Play:** a pan, a zoom or a double tap holds the animation while the hand is on the map, and Play goes on by itself once it lets go.
- **Pause answers at once**, also on a phone at 30 steps a second.

## The map on a phone

- **Fingers in every tool:** one finger pans and two pinch in Navigate, Point and Region alike, and the pinch keeps the map's size and equal degrees on both axes. In Region, a tap and then a touch that drags draws the box. A double tap frames the whole map, also while Play runs fast.
- **Upright:** the map is as tall as the grid needs, with the time bar always on screen under it. The tools follow in the order a finger uses them (extraction, the preview of the series, their list, the inspector), and a thin bar at the foot of the map names them and scrolls to them with a tap.
- **On its side:** the map and its time bar take the whole height, and the tools are in a panel beside the map that a toolbar button shows and hides.
- Turning the phone keeps a whole map; the **Map** button in the Data sheet closes the sheet onto the map; the extraction progress bar is drawn large enough to see on an iPhone.

## 3D views

In 3D, 2D + t and the 3D state animation, the mouse and the fingers now do the same thing:

- A double click or a quick double tap goes back to the opening view; Home, the view presets, Ctrl+Z and a rebuild treat the orthographic zoom the way they treat the perspective one, and every 3D view shares one home camera.
- A middle or Alt drag zooms in either projection; a sideways wheel no longer rolls the camera.
- On a touch screen, a slow double tap then a drag pans the view in the desktop layout too.
- An orbit released outside the plot is kept: in the 3D animation the view no longer jumped back when playback resumed.

## Desktop capabilities

- Fully local, offline application and bundled processing libraries.
- External documentation links open in the operating system's default browser.
- No classic Electron menu or secondary Electron browser windows.
