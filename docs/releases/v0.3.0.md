# Time Series Explorer v0.3.0

Signals you can hear, filters designed from a specification rather than typed as coefficients, Micro-Cap and Apple Lossless files that open on their own, and an interface that no longer recomputes what it already knows.

## Downloads

- **Windows 10/11 x64:** installer and portable EXE.
- **macOS Intel:** DMG installer and ZIP package.
- **macOS Apple silicon:** DMG installer and ZIP package.
- **Linux x86_64:** DEB installer and AppImage package.
- **SHA256SUMS files:** checksums for verifying the downloaded packages.

The executables are currently unsigned. Verify that the file was downloaded from the official Time Series Explorer download button and compare its SHA-256 checksum before running it.

- **Windows:** SmartScreen may show an **Unknown publisher** warning. Choose *More info* → *Run anyway*.
- **macOS:** Gatekeeper refuses an unsigned, un-notarized app with *"Time Series Explorer" cannot be opened*. Open **System Settings → Privacy & Security**, scroll to the message naming the app, and click **Open Anyway**; then confirm in the dialog that follows. On macOS Sequoia this panel is the only route — right-click → Open no longer offers the override.

## Listen to a signal

The 🔊 button in a panel's toolbar opens a transport strip under the chart, and the curve the panel draws can be heard, with a playhead moving over it.

- **Playable means uniformly sampled, not "is an audio file".** A filtered signal, a resampled one, a derived formula — anything on an even grid plays. Comparing a recording with its filtered version is the point, so that comparison had to be possible without exporting anything.
- **One player, one clock.** Pressing play in another panel stops the first and the first keeps its position, which is what lets two versions of the same sound be judged against each other. What belongs to a panel is where it is listening from; what belongs to the app is the sound itself.
- **Click the curve to move the playhead.** The instant you clicked is the instant it plays from, and the playhead follows the panel through a zoom, a pan and a redraw.
- **The level is not a control.** Every signal is normalised so its peak lands at -1 dBFS, because a volume slider on data whose units are volts or degrees invites a reading that is not there.
- **What it cannot do, it says.** A sample rate outside what the audio hardware accepts is named with the limit it exceeded, rather than failing silently.
- Removing a curve from a panel no longer blanks the panel or cuts the sound, closing the strip takes the playhead with it, and a signal recomputed mid-playback is heard as it is now, not as it was when play was pressed.

## New files

- **Micro-Cap numeric output** (`.tno`, `.ano`, `.dno`) opens directly: the banner and the Limits/Stepping prose are skipped, each waveform table becomes signals named as they were printed — `v(C1)`, `DB(V(2))`, `PD(R3)` — with their units, and the first column is the file's X axis. Parameter stepping repeats the table once per combination, so each signal becomes a tree group with one entry per run, labelled by the values that actually differ (`C1=10u`, `V1=12`). Output renamed to `.txt` or `.out` is recognised by its banner rather than its extension.
- **Apple Lossless is decoded by the app itself.** An iPhone voice memo recorded on the "Lossless" setting is ALAC in an `.m4a`, and no browser but Safari ships a decoder for it, so the file simply did not open. The decoder is now ours — adaptive Rice coding, an adaptive FIR predictor, one stereo decorrelation — and it returns the samples bit-exact at the file's own rate, verified frame for frame against ffmpeg on a 1,290,240-frame recording.
- **WhatsApp voice messages open.** They download as `.mp4`, which is the same container `.m4a` names; only the extension list was refusing them. Accepting it raises the case the extension usually means, so a file holding video and no sound track is now refused by saying exactly that, instead of failing deep inside a decoder.

## Design a filter instead of typing one

The Digital filter tool gains a **Design from a specification** mode: family (Butterworth, Chebyshev I, Chebyshev II, Bessel), response (low-pass, high-pass, band-pass, band-stop), order, and cut-off in Hz. The sample rate is read off the source variable's own time axis; an irregular axis refuses the design with the reason spelled out rather than quietly assuming one.

The design takes the textbook route — analog prototype, frequency transformation, pre-warped bilinear transform — so the coefficients match MATLAB's `butter`, `cheby1`, `cheby2` and `besself` and SciPy's `iirfilter`. They run as a cascade of second-order sections, because at high order with a low cut-off the rounding in a single polynomial alone is enough to put a pole outside the unit circle. The expanded `b` and `a` are still written into the coefficient boxes, read-only, for reading and copying. Typed coefficients keep working exactly as before.

## Derived datasets

- **A cross-correlation tool.** Two signals of one file in, `r_xy` as a function of lag out, on a lag axis of its own in the file's time unit. The convention is MATLAB's `xcorr` and SciPy's `correlate`, and the message after Create reads the sign out in words, so a peak at a positive lag does not have to be interpreted from memory. All four normalisations are available, mean removal is on by default, and holes remove pairs rather than poisoning every lag that touches them.
- **A resample carries its recipe.** A derived file now knows where it came from: it is listed under its source in the variable tree, indented in the files list with a *derived* badge, given a row in the Transformations table, edited in place, recomputed when its source is reloaded, saved to a session as the recipe rather than as rows, and closed together with its source.
- A resample is plotted alongside its source instead of on a new panel, and the derived-dataset tools got the panel, values view and busy state that the rest of the Data Tools already had.

## Derived variables

The formula language gains trigonometry (`sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `asin`, `acos`, `atan`, in radians, with the `arcsin` spellings accepted), the hyperbolic three (`sinh`, `cosh`, `tanh`), the shape pair `sign` and `step` — the ordinary signum and the unit step, so `x*step(x)` rectifies and `step(time - 5)` gates — and variadic `min` and `max`.

It also gains the named constants `pi` and `e`, so a formula can be written the way it is written on paper: `sin(2*pi*1000*time)`. A file's own variables always win: a name is looked up as a constant only when the loaded file has nothing by that name, so no saved formula can change value because this exists. `math.pi` and `math.e` are the way out when a file does shadow them.

## Faster

- **A spectrum is not recomputed when nothing it depends on changed.** Hiding a curve, closing one, and switching the amplitude scale to dB each used to bring up the "Computing FFT" pill and spend seconds arriving at a spectrum the panel already had. Spectra are cached per panel against what the transform actually reads; dB is a remap of the result, not a reason to transform again.
- **A file's sampling step is measured once.** Applying a filter to a minute of audio at 48 kHz froze the interface for about twenty seconds while the filter itself took 64 ms — the time went to measuring the axis thirty-eight times over. The median is now found by selection instead of a sort, and each axis is measured once and shared by the filter, the cross-correlation, the resampler and the interpolator.
- **WebGL is chosen from the points drawn, not from the length of the file.** A long file decimates to about two thousand points before anything is drawn, so asking for a WebGL context on every redraw bought nothing and cost seconds of blocking per update.
- The FFT time pane is redrawn at the resolution actually visible.

## Interface

- **The mouse wheel adjusts a slider after a short hover.** A notch moves one percent of the slider's span — a full sweep in about a hundred notches — never less than the control's own step and always rounded to a whole number of them, so grids like odd-only windows or whole frames survive. Shift refines it.
- Reload shows the loading overlay while it re-reads the file.
- The browser's Open dialog shows every file, and the desktop dialog offers **All files** second.
- Data-tool warnings dismiss themselves, and a notice about a file leaves with the file.
- "Computing" is a card that goes green when the result lands, and the audio note and the loading pill are said in the app's language.

## Fixes

- The FFT legend stays in panel order when a curve is switched off.
- A cross-correlation's lag axis is kept out of the time link, while a panel that mixes ordinary signals with one stays in it.
- The Micro-Cap file badge is shortened to the brand name.
- Numbers in the data-tool number fields have room on the right again.

## Desktop capabilities

- Fully local, offline application and bundled processing libraries.
- External documentation links open in the operating system's default browser.
- No classic Electron menu or secondary Electron browser windows.
